1Dataset

Each pair is built from one real clip: the named object is segmented with a text-prompted video segmenter and inpainted out, and its sound is separated out of the soundtrack. A pair is used in both directions — original → edited for removal, edited → original for addition. 24,444 training / 1,389 validation / 1,425 test pairs; the test set covers 374 object descriptions.

6Failure cases

Typical failures of the dataset and of our model, grouped by type. They were found with the audio-visual judge, which is asked whether the object is visible, whether it is audible, and whether sound and picture are in sync; its explanation is quoted under each example.

6.1Dataset failures

Errors in how the pairs were built; they bound what any model trained or scored on this data can reach.

6.1.1Sound separation leaves the object audible

The separated soundtrack of the edited clip still contains the object's sound.

F1.1.1

Object: idling engine

JudgeThe idling engine sound is audibly present and remains consistent without interruption across the clip.
F1.1.2

Object: drumming

JudgeThe sound of drumming is clearly audible and remains consistent throughout the clip.

6.1.2Inpainting leaves the object (or a remnant) visible

The inpainted picture of the edited clip still shows the object or a recognisable remnant.

F1.2.1

Object: truck engine

JudgeThe engine is clearly visible and remains consistent in its appearance and operation across the entire clip.
F1.2.2

Object: toilet

JudgeThe toilet is consistently visible as the camera pulls back and focuses.

6.1.3The named object is heard but never seen in the original clip

The prompt names a sound source that is off-screen, or does not match what the picture shows.

F1.3.1

Object: motorcycle engine

JudgeThe video shows a car engine, not a motorcycle engine; the target is entirely missing.
F1.3.2

Object: engine

JudgeThe target engine is not visually present at any point in the clip.

6.2Removal failures

Our model, text prompt only, on clips whose input clearly shows and sounds the object.

6.2.1The object is left in place

The output is close to the input: the object is still fully visible.

F2.1.1

Remove truck engine

JudgeThe engine remains visible throughout the entire clip.
F2.1.2

Remove toilet

JudgeThe target object (toilet) remains visible as a close-up surface for the entire duration.

6.2.2A ghost or blurred remnant stays behind

The object is only partly erased and leaves a blurred or ghost-like shape.

F2.2.1

Remove train

JudgeA massive, moving artifact retaining the shape and color of the train is visible for the entire clip.
F2.2.2

Remove train

JudgeA large, moving ghost image of the train remains visible on the tracks for the entire clip.

6.2.3The object leaves the picture but its sound stays

The visual removal succeeds while the soundtrack keeps the object's sound, although the reference audio is clean.

F2.3.1

Remove electronic device

JudgeThe buzzing sound of the electronic device remains clearly audible for the entire duration.
F2.3.2

Remove motorcycle engine

JudgeThe sound of a motorcycle engine is clearly audible continuously from the beginning to the end of the clip.

6.3Addition failures

Our model, text prompt plus first frame, on clips whose input has neither the object nor its sound.

6.3.1The object drifts away from the first frame (identity or shape changes)

The object starts from the given first frame but its shape or identity changes over time.

F3.1.1

Add cargo ship

JudgeThe ship structure is highly distorted and morphs continuously, lacking consistency.
F3.1.2

Add animal

JudgeThe animal severely distorts and loses its initial identity, becoming an unrecognizable shape starting around 0.5s.

6.3.2The object fades out after the first frame

The object is present at the first frame and then disappears.

F3.2.1

Add male vocalist

JudgeNo singing action is visible, and the person's head disappears entirely at 00:01.000.
F3.2.2

Add canine

JudgeThe dog arbitrarily disappears and blends into the floor texture at 00:01.000 and 00:04.000.

6.3.3Object and sound are both added, but out of sync

Both streams contain the object, but sound events do not line up with the visible action.

F3.3.1

Add barking dog

JudgeThe audible barks do not match the visible actions of the dog, which appears to be sniffing or eating rather than barking, especially noticeable around 00:03 and 00:04.
F3.3.2

Add rooster

JudgeThe rooster crowing sound occurs between 00:01.8 and 00:03.0, but the visible rooster's beak remains closed and it does not perform the physical action of crowing.
